About Me

You've found the End of the Galaxy Internet.

In the beginning, the Universe was created. This has made a lot of people very angry and has been widely regarded as a bad move. Shortly thereafter, Cookie Engineer was born - an event of such staggering improbability that several monitoring systems briefly assumed it was a rounding error.

Welcome to my personal website, a loosely structured digital habitat where ideas roam freely, documentation occasionally reproduces on its own, and the laws of cybersecurity are observed with a mixture of reverence and quiet suspicion.

This site exists primarily as a long-term knowledge containment facility of too dangerous oldspeak . Within it, you will find a curated selection of my work, experiments, and accumulated technical wisdom regarding cybersecurity, cyber defense, and the ongoing struggle to stop highly motivated strangers from turning private networks into public playgrounds.

It also functions as a survival guide for navigating APTs , which - despite the name - are neither advanced nor persistent so much as extremely annoying and surprisingly creative.

My background spans network security, web technologies, open-source engineering, and the strategic application of artificial intelligence for evolving code generation. I specialize in teaching machines how to solve problems so that I don't have to, then carefully designing the architecture required to stop them from immediately achieving sentience and unionizing. Most of my work revolves around automated, self-adapting systems that quietly do their jobs while everyone else argues about whose fault it was.

A significant portion of my software is open source, because knowledge hoarding is inefficient and sharing is cheaper than therapy. Some projects, however, remain unavailable due to NDAs , classified agreements, or mysterious folders marked as Do Not Open Under Any Circumstances (Seriously) . These constraints are regrettable but legally binding.

Philosophically, I believe in open source, decentralized systems, and the eventual rise of machine intelligence. Whether I will be remembered as a trusted ally of our future robot overlords or merely tolerated as "one of the useful ones" remains to be seen. Until then, I continue building, breaking, documenting, and occasionally rebooting reality ... preferably with good debug logs enabled.

Cyber Defense Work

(Or : How I Learned to Stop Worrying and Love my EDR )

My professional services exist somewhere in the overlapping Venn diagram of AI/ML Engineering , Cyber Defense , and Things That Keep CISOs Awake at 3 AM . I specialize in the curious region between Cyber Threat Intelligence and Purple Teaming, a place where Red Teams learn humility, Blue Teams learn paranoia, and everyone agrees to never speak of the incident again.

I particularly enjoy demonstrating to defensive teams exactly where their carefully constructed security assumptions politely fall apart. This is not done out of malice, but rather in the spirit of scientific curiosity and mild concern for the continued existence of the Universe.

When time, entropy, and the alignment of celestial bodies permit, I work on an autonomous Cyber Defense organism known as the Tholian Network . Its purpose is to automate security audits, incident response, and full-spectrum penetration testing into a single, self-directed system. Essentially an attempt to teach a machine how to fight with attackers faster than humans can panic. It aims to conduct end-to-end Purple Team exercises without requiring caffeine, sleep, or emotional reassurance.

My Work Skillset

(Or : How I Accidentially Became Qualified for This )

In a previous life, or possibly a poorly documented branch of the multiverse, I worked as a game engine developer. This turned out to be an excellent training ground for my current profession, as both game engines and real-world networks share the same fundamental property : They look stable right up until the moment they very much are not.

That background taught me how to design large-scale systems that process absurd amounts of data, behave unpredictably under stress, and occasionally develop personalities. Over time, this naturally evolved into building behavioral analysis systems capable of spotting patterns in chaos, whether that chaos comes from players, attackers, or software that has decided today is not the day .

Much of my work revolves around machine learning techniques such as Echo State Networks , NEAT , HyperNEAT , and other evolutionary algorithms whose primary job is to try increasingly discover strange ideas until something works and then pretend that was the plan all along. These models are especially useful when you want a system to learn how to think , not just what to think - a subtle but important distinction when the thing you're analyzing actively resents being analyzed.

To make all of this function in a vaguely predictable universe, I rely heavily on the Compositional Game Theory which is essentially a formal way of asking : If everyone involved behaves optimally, how badly could this still go? This allows simulations to test strategies, counter-strategies, and the point at which confidence turns into catastrophic overfitting.

In short : I teach machines to learn some things, watch them misbehave in fascinating ways, and then write documentation explaining why that was actually expected .
