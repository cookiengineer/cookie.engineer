Anet A8

Overview

GitHub Repository | GitLab Mirror
Download

The Anet A8 is a cheap acryl-based printer that was very cheap on Alibaba when I bought it. It's a hacker's printer because you probably will spend a lot of time tinkering on it and improving the print quality of it.

I started a repository wherein I documented my upgrades and modifications for it, so that other people can use them too. It's a pretty nice printer, actually, with a very good precision considering that it only has an acrylic frame.

Parts List

  • 2mm / 4mm PTFE tube (min 50cm length)
  • 2.5mm fibre-rubber GT2 timing belts (min 200cm length)
  • 2x 4mm pneumatic adapter (tube feed)
  • 2x M5x50 screws
  • 2x M5 self-sealing nuts
  • 2x NEMA 17 stepper motors

Note : The PT FE tube needs to have an inner diameter of 2mm and an outer diameter of 4mm. If you want to use bigger filament diameters, change the pneumatic adapter sizes accordingly, too.

The Z-axis stepper motors should be replaced with stronger NEMA 17 stepper motors, as many users had troubles with a stuck Z-axis motor driver that was too weak to lift the X-carriage.

All parts should be printed with 90% honeycomb infill . 0.12mm layer height for the X-belt holder and the Z-wobble fixes, and 0.2mm layer height for all other parts.

X-Axis Fixes

  • Print the x-belt holder part
  • Print the x-belt tensioner part

Install the belt tensioners and the belt holder.

The X-Tensioner requires 2x M5 screws with at least 35mm length with matching self-sealing nuts. In order to have some space left for not-so-tight belts, use the M5x50 screws for this.

Use proper fiber-rubber GT2 timing belts, not the plastic OEM ones that came with the printer. It is best to buy a GT2 belt kit, so that you have bigger cog heels that you can use for the Bowden Extruder ugrade.

Y-Axis Fixes

  • Print the y-belt holder top part
  • Print the y-belt holder bottom part
  • Print the y-belt tensioner part
  • Print the y-tensioner adapter part
  • Print the y-tensioner holder part

Install the belt tensioners and the belt holder.

Reuse the same Y-Tensioner OEM screws that came with the Anet A8 printer.

Z-Axis Fixes

  • Print the z-wobble fix part 2 times
  • Print the t-corner part 2 times

Install the z-wobble fix and the T-corners.

Z-Axis Stepper Motors

The OEM Stepper motors are too weak to properly lift the X-carriage. In my case, they broke down around 10 hours after printing. The typical symptom is that the Z-axis motors make a loud shredding noise and/or only one of them is reacting because the Z-motor driver on the main board already broke down.

To avoid this, it is recommended to replace the Z-axis motors with larger ones. I suggest the 48mm NEMA 17 motors, as they are quite a bit larger than the OEM ones but still fit well inside the OEM frame.

There is a list of commonly used NEMA 17 motors available from the RepRap Wiki .

Wiring is a little fiddle work, because the wiring on the Anet's main board is using a cross-paired blue and black wire, whle the outer two wires are connected normal.

Please try this out yourself first, as every stepper motor is wired differently and every stepper cable seems to have different colors for each cable pair.

It takes a while to do this, so grab a cup of tea and stay calm while doing it. If it doesn't work on the first couple tries, it's best to do this another day. I speak from experience.

Glass Plate with Clip Holders

Install the glass plate with clip holders, for improved print quality and easy reuse of the glass plate after cleaning the remnant plastic parts.

Install the clip holders so that they don't conflict with the 0/0/0 (Home All) coordinate of the extruder (see photo).

When using the glass plate, it is recommended to use a simple glue stick to make things sticky and to print on it as the glass plate itself is too slick for the initial layers of PLA or ABS material.

Some folks recommend to increase the print temperature, but this will cause more problems than it solves. Just use the glue stick instead.

Heatbed Leveling

Leveling the heatbed is pretty painful, as the Anet A8 doesn't come with a way to autolevel it using sensors in an automated manner. It is recommended to use a water level for that. Make sure to have a flat, leveled underground under the printer.

Afterwards follow this instruction video to level the heatbed correctly.

Take your time; correctly leveling the heatbed is 50% of the print quality. It's seriously important to be done right.

Download and Setup Cura with the Anet A8 Printer

The Cura software that comes with the USB Stick (or on the microSD card) is outdated. Install the newest stable one from ultimaker.com .

Important Note for Linux users: Pretty much all packaged builds are broken, use the self-executing AppImage from the ultimaker website.

Start the Cura software, add a new printer (which is the welcome dialog on first start).

Select Custom > FDM Printer . The detailed printer settings are: 

// Printer Settings

x (width):  220mm
y (depth):  220mm
z (height): 240mm // use 235mm if heatbed has glass plate

build plate shape: rectangular
machine center:    NOT zero
heated bed:        yes

Printhead Settings

x min: 0mm
y min: 0mm
x max: 0mm
y max: 0mm

gantry height: 0mm
nozzle size:   0.4mm

Then in the next step, set the GCode Flavor to RepRap (Marlin) .

Enter the following Start GCode:

G21            ;metric values
G90            ;absolute positioning
M82            ;set extruder to absolute mode
M107           ;start with the fan off
G28 X0 Y0      ;move X/Y to min endstops
G28 Z0         ;move Z to min endstops
G1 Z15.0 F9000 ;move the platform down 15mm
G92 E0         ;zero the extruded length
G1 F200 E3     ;extrude 3mm of feed stock
G92 E0         ;zero the extruded length again
G1 F9000
M117 Printing...

Enter the following End GCode:

M104 S0                      ;extruder heater off
M140 S0                      ;bed heater off
G91                          ;relative positioning
G1 E-1 F300                  ;retract the filament to release pressure
G1 Z+0.5 E-5 X-20 Y-20 F9000 ;move Z up a bit
G28 X0 Y0                    ;move X/Y to min endstops
G90                          ;absolute positioning
G1 Y190 F9000                ;(optional) move heatbed to front
M84                          ;steppers off

Now go to Settings > Profiles > Manage Profiles and import the Anet A8 Cura Profile .

It will lead to the correct settings that you need to make the Anet A8 print awesome results.

You can verify that your Anet A8 machine is correctly set up by comparing the machine_instances folder inside the provided Z IP file with the one located at ~/.local/share/cura/machine_instances .

Print Calibration Cube

Now it's time to test your settings. Make sure the printer is ready, has its filament fed in, is correctly leveled and ready for a test run.

The test run consists of the typical Calibration Cube model, while setting infill to 50% and printing it with support structures.

If the cube is not 20mm in all dimensions or its shape does not look like in the picture, you probably have a not correctly leveled heatbed and you should re-level it.

Spool Holder Upgrade

The OEM spool holder has a lot of tension, and before we can continue to ugrade to a Bowden extruder printer, we need to print the better spool holder.

  • Print the spool holder bolt
  • Print the spool holder nut

Install the spool holder. Make sure that the feeding of the extruder motor is not hindered by other parts that are in the way and that the filament is a straight line between spool holder and the extruder motor.

Bowden Extruder Upgrade

The standard Anet A8 printer design comes with an extruder motor that is mounted directly above the extruder. This causes more weight on the X-carriage and X-axis which has to be moved by the X-axis motor and belt system.

The bowden extruder upgrade allows the extruder motor to be moved atop the frame, so that it both stabilizes the frame and allows the filament to be fed through a PTFE tube.

  • Print the motor block part
  • Print the motor mount part
  • Print the extruder button part
  • Print the pneumatic adapter part 2 times (1 replacement part, you will need it)

Install the motor parts as shown in the photos above, and do the same for the extruder. It is necessary to have the PT FE tube and the pneumatic feeds available.

An important note here is that PT FE tube feeds have inch measurements, and not metric ones. All pneumatic hardware uses inch measurements, as a side fact. It is important to buy a 3/8" tube feed ( ~9.51mm ), as M10 ( ~10.18mm ) would be too big to fit into the adapter.

If you have an M6 thread cutter, it is recommended to use it for the smaller side of the pneumatic adapter, so that less stress is on the PLA (or ABS) material when being screwed into the metal motor block on the X-carriage.

Mount the extruder motor on the top left of the printer's frame, so that the PT FE tube-side points to the front-left direction.

Make sure that the cables and the PT FE tube don't conflict in the whole movement area of the X-carriage.

The extruder motor will probably require a bigger cog wheel, because the OEM cog wheel is a little too small to grab and feed the filament properly.

Use the one that came with the GT2 belt kit that you bought in the first step.

Z-Endstops Upgrade

The OEM Z-Endstops are painful to use because they require a lot of recalibration The provided Z-Endstop is adjustable, so that no matter how your heatbed looks like, you can simply adjust only one screw without having to always recalibrate the endstop itself.

  • Print the endstop mount part
  • Print the endstop holder part
  • Print the endstop cap part

Install the Z-Endstop. If you reset the position of the printer to the Home All position in the printer's firmware menu, you can listen to the sound of the endstop.

It has to be mounted in a way that it's directly clicking and in the Off position when you move the Z-Endstop adjustment screw by around 30deg .

Use a couple of M3 washers to adjust the distance, so that the cap is able to hit the endstop correctly.

Heatbed Cable Connectors

Most people build up the Anet A8 printer similar to the Prusa i3 printer, where the heatbed is mounted in a way so that the cables of the heatbed lead to the side and not towards the back.

At first glimpse the Y-cable chain is a well-thought fix for having nice and clean cables. But that causes a burned cable connector because it will receive too much physical stress.

I recommend to resolder the headbed, and to mount the heatbed in a way so that the cables lead towards the back and NOT towards the side. This way the cables and soldered connections don't have too much physical stress on them.

The procedure is fairly simple, even if you are new to soldering. Just be patient and remove any excess solder you may use. If the old solder doesn't come off, put a bit of new solder on it and it will melt immediately.

Try to remove the old wires and pull the connectors off with pliers, they will come off with a bit of force. Desolder and remove the outer pins, so that you can still use the inner piece of the original connector for the thermistor.

You should use an higher-gauge wire to connect the heatbed, the one that come with the printer is too thin to carry the 10A used by the bed. A thicker cable means that the wire will stay colder when transferring current.

Remember to insert heat-shrink tubes before soldering and keep them distant from the ends or it will shrink immediately. Put the shrink tubes between the wire and the metal frame of the bed. It shouldn't be an issue if the wire touches the coating at the bottom of the heatbed, but it's better to keep them separated.

DO NOT connect both the + (positive) or both the - (negative) pins, as this will make the heatbed be able to take up to 20A current from the motherboard, and will lead to a burnt motherboard. Only connect a single one of the outer pins.
